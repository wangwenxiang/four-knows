Worth noting that to this day, base LLMs (no test time compute) *still* perform poorly on the ARC 1 benchmark from 2019 (on unseen tasks) despite continued massive scaling (~100,000x since 2019). We can say that without the switch to the test-time compute paradigm, AI would still not be capable of the kind of advanced reasoning that sota systems display today. Scaling the single-pass, static, next-token prediction paradigm of the GPT-2 through GPT-4 era was running into a capability asymptote. To bypass this plateau, test-time adaptation was a necessary evolutionary step.

值得注意的是，直到今天，基础 LLM（没有测试时计算）在 2019 年的 ARC 1 基准上（面对未见过的任务）表现仍然很差，尽管规模持续大幅扩大（自 2019 年以来约 100,000 倍）。可以说，如果没有转向测试时计算范式，AI 仍无法具备当今 sota 系统展现出的那种高级推理能力。GPT-2 到 GPT-4 时代那种单次前向、静态、下一个 token 预测范式的扩展，已经逼近能力渐近线。要突破这个平台期，测试时适应是必要的演进步骤。

Gary Marcus@GaryMarcus

The “AGI-is-near” community keeps committing the same logical fallacy over and over; I have seen it at least half a dozen times today alone. Every time there’s an advance, I see the same error. Here’s how the fallacy works. 1. Someone pretends that all cognition is created equally. (Totally untrue.) 2. Whenever AI achieves success on some form of (fancy) cognition they want you to believe that success on all forms of AI is imminent. I include three prominent examples from today below, each promising that some grand universal can be solved (“science”, “every discipline”, “every problem [people] face in life”), simply because there was an advance in a particular domain (today, math, other times coding, etc). You don’t have to be a cognitive psychologist to realize that this inference just doesn’t follow. We all know that expertise in math doesn’t guarantee genius in all domains. We all know, for example, people who are great at math or physics or programming but struggle with writing or understanding human relationships (and conversely great writers who are weak at math), etc. It’s common to be good at some forms of cognition and not others. Expertise in one domain does not at all guarantee expertise in all or even most domains. That’s precisely *why* people like Gardner and Sternberg developed multidimensional theories of intelligence, why the SAT tests math separately from verbal, etc. Astra is (apparently - we still haven’t seen the methodology) great at math, or at least some forms of math, but that does not mean that it will avoid hallucinations or solve the reliability problems other GenAI systems have. It doesn’t even mean it will be able to read a PDF properly. It doesn’t mean it will be able to follow hard rules either. (Which should terrify you.) In fact, the performance we saw today doesn’t even mean it can write a decent math proof; to the contrary, the mathematician @henryquantum has already given an example where clarity in the proof was lacking. Whatever you do, please don’t get suckered into the “all cognition is alike” fallacy. It’s not all alike. Astra is obviously good at some problems; but that doesn’t mean it will be good at problems that are hard to formalize. It doesn’t mean it will be magic. It doesn’t mean it’s AGI or ASI or any of that. It’s *very* impressive. But I see no reason whatsoever to think it is AGI let alone ASI. If it can score even a 5/10 on my 2024 bet with Miles Brundage I will be surprised. Enough with this fallacy.

“AGI 即将到来”群体一再犯同一个逻辑谬误；光是今天我就至少见过六次。每当有进展出现，我都会看到同样的错误。 这个谬误是这样运作的。 1. 有人假装所有认知都是一样的。（完全不对。） 2. 每当 AI 在某种（高级）认知形式上取得成功，他们就想让你相信，AI 在所有形式上的成功都近在眼前。 我在下面列出今天的三个典型例子，它们都承诺某个宏大的普遍目标可以被解决（“科学”、“每个学科”、“[人们]生活中面对的每个问题”），仅仅因为某个特定领域取得了进展（今天是数学，其他时候是编程等）。 你不必是认知心理学家也能意识到，这种推论根本站不住脚。我们都知道，数学专长并不能保证在所有领域都是天才。 例如，我们都认识一些数学、物理或编程很强，但在写作或理解人际关系方面吃力的人（反过来也有数学薄弱的优秀作家），等等。 擅长某些认知形式而不擅长另一些，是很常见的。一个领域的专长完全不能保证在所有甚至大多数领域都有专长。 这正是 Gardner 和 Sternberg 等人提出多维智能理论的原因，也是 SAT 将数学和语文分开测试的原因，等等。 Astra（显然——我们还没看到方法论）很擅长数学，或至少某些形式的数学，但这并不意味着它会避免幻觉，或解决其他 GenAI 系统存在的可靠性问题。甚至不意味着它能正确读取 PDF。也不意味着它能遵循严格规则。（这应该让你感到害怕。） 事实上，我们今天看到的表现甚至不意味着它能写出一篇像样的数学证明；相反，数学家 @henryquantum 已经给出一个例子，说明其证明缺乏清晰性。 无论如何，请不要被“所有认知都一样”的谬误骗了。它们并不都一样。 Astra 显然擅长某些问题；但这并不意味着它会擅长难以形式化的问题。不意味着它是魔法。不意味着它是 AGI 或 ASI 或诸如此类的东西。 它非常令人印象深刻。但我完全看不出有什么理由认为它是 AGI，更不用说 ASI 了。如果它能在我 2024 年与 Miles Brundage 的赌约中拿到哪怕 5/10，我都会感到惊讶。 别再犯这个谬误了。

To address the limits of deep learning and avoid stalling, the field of AI started by applying patch (1), which started being demoed 9 months later in December 2024 and has now become completely ubiquitous. However, long term, it is simply inevitable that AI will move to patch (2).

为了解决深度学习的局限并避免停滞，AI 领域一开始采用了补丁 (1)，并在 9 个月后的 2024 年 12 月开始展示，如今已变得无处不在。然而，从长远看，AI 转向补丁 (2) 是不可避免的。

François Chollet@fchollet

There are essentially two main options to remedy this: 1. Find ways to perform active inference, so that the model adapts its learned program in contact with a new data distribution at test time. Would likely lead to some meaningful progress, but it isn't the ultimate solution, more of an incremental improvement. 2. Change the training mechanism to something more robust than SGD, such as the MDL principle. This would pretty much require moving away from deep learning (curve fitting) altogether and embracing discrete program search instead (which I have advocated for many years as a way to tackle reasoning problems...)

本质上有两个主要办法可以补救： 1. 寻找进行主动推理的方法，让模型在测试时接触新的数据分布时调整其学到的程序。这可能会带来一些有意义的进展，但不是最终解决方案，更像是渐进式改进。 2. 将训练机制换成比 SGD 更稳健的东西，比如 MDL 原则。这基本上需要彻底离开深度学习（曲线拟合），转而采用离散程序搜索（多年来我一直主张用这种方式解决推理问题……）
