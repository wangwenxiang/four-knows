现在很多 Agent 自进化，大致走两条路： 一条改模型权重，一条改 Harness。 这篇 Evo² 直接把两条路接了起来。 系统里，同一个模型同时扮演两个角色： Proposer 负责修改 Harness，Reflector 负责看执行结果、分析哪里改对了、哪里还需要修。 关键在下一步。 这些 Harness 修改和真实执行结果，会继续拿回来训练 Proposer 和 Reflector。模型变得更会改 Harness，新的 Harness 又产生更好的训练经验，形成一个持续循环。 结果是 Evo² 在 Polyglot 上达到 82.1%，最强 baseline 是 62.6%；SWE-bench Verified 达到 48.3%，相比 baseline 的 41.0% 继续提升。 而且进化出来的 Harness 可以直接迁移到其他 benchmark，不需要重新搜索。固定 GPT-5-mini 权重时，仅靠这套 Harness 进化流程，也把表现从 24.4% 提升到 48.3%。 这篇真正往前推的一步，是让「改进者」本身也进入了学习循环。 以后自进化 Agent 可能会越来越像两个互相促进的系统： 一边积累更好的外部运行结构，一边训练出更会修改这些结构的模型。 当「怎么让自己变强」也开始被持续训练，self-evolution 才真正有了第二层递归。 📎 arxiv: https://t.co/pfHcHHL7BF

In our health workstream at Meta we did a project where we wanted to see what research papers medical experts could write using a Muse Wonder coding scaffold that could access a database of 5M electronic health records we licensed from a vendor. The goal is that with 5M patients in this database, AI could do research to answer arbitrary questions like “did people who take statin have higher rates of diabetes later in life”? The thing we were betting on here is on what happens when you put human experts, a strong coding model, and access to data all together We worked with a small group of medical experts to help them use this AI tool and the punchline is that they all found that this setup enabled a much faster pace of health research. They generated ten manuscripts in around two weeks, with one researcher (Paddy Ssentongo) generating three. Throughout the process, they collaborated with each other to review manuscripts and write skill files to help our coding scaffold improve at doing research. The medical experts liked how quickly they could test different hypotheses and choose the best ones to continue working on Health research is pretty unique in that it is super important to be able to interpret noisy data and have taste to know which variables are important, which was still a significant value add from human researchers. Given that medicine is one of the oldest fields in human history and it is ultimately about humans, keeping a substantial human component in this type of research is probably important. In the near future I see AI primarily as a tool to facilitate human-led research. With the right scaffold I believe health researchers can be 10x or even 100x more productive using AI tools that have appropriate access to the wealth of data that the world has collected about human health One way to see this work is through the broader lens of AI for science. You can think of science as two parts: (1) collecting raw data about the world, and (2) synthesizing that raw data into understanding. In this project we already had existing data about the world and we aimed to accelerate part (2) using AI. Eventually, I imagine a world where we have such great AI tools that the time to synthesize raw data into understanding is driven towards zero. In that world, science will be substantially faster and most of the work will be focused on collecting the right raw data via physical experiments We did this in a pretty short amount of time (which was the point) and I think the future of scientific research will be more faster and more exciting than ever
