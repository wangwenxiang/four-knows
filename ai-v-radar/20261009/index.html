这篇论文很有意思！现在连 RSI 都开始有自己的「实验场」了。 Evolvent AI 和 NUS 这篇论文提出 RSIGym，一个专门让 Agent 研究「怎么改进 AI」的实验环境。 它把训练、推理、数据生成、评测和沙盒都封装成服务。Research Agent 可以直接调用这些能力，然后不断循环： 分析问题 → 改数据 / 训练配置 / Harness → 训练 → 评测 → 根据结果继续修改。 更有意思的是，它把自我改进拆成三种模式： Data 只允许改训练数据； Harness 只改模型外的运行系统； Joint 则把数据、训练配置和 Harness 一起开放给 Agent。 作者还提出 RSI-Index，衡量系统在 SWE-bench、Terminal-Bench、AIME、GPQA 和 SkillsBench 上填平了多少「距离满分的差距」。 在每个 benchmark $500 的预算下，6 个前沿研究模型都能让初始系统变强。 其中 Opus 5 的 RSI-Index 最高，达到 0.4809；SWE-bench Verified 从 17.67% 提升到 50.33%，AIME 从 31.67% 提升到 97.78%。 还有一个挺有意思的结果，只改 Harness 就能带来明显提升，而单独增加训练数据的效果很有限。 看完这篇，我觉得 RSI 正在从一个很抽象的概念，慢慢变成可以真正做实验的系统问题。 接下来值得看的，就是这些「改进」能不能被继承，并继续帮助下一轮产生更好的改进。 📎 arxiv: https://t.co/5Hgl0rga8F

Scientists have sequenced the genomes of thousands of species. But for most of them, nobody knows *where the genes are* Today we're releasing Carbon-A, an open model that finds genes directly in DNA. We used it to create a database of 566 million candidate genes across 22,617 species, from fungi to mammals (that we are sharing as well) Why bother? A few reasons 1. Elephants rarely get cancer. Part of the explanation turned up in their genes: extra copies of a tumor-suppressor gene humans have only one of. You can't ask that kind of question about a species until someone, or some tool, has found its genes. 2. The first GLP-1 drug was based on a peptide from Gila monster venom. Nobody would have put the Gila monster on a priority list. Same for wild relatives of crops, which carry resistance to drought and disease. Many of these species have never been annotated. 3. Most of what we know about genes comes from about a dozen model species, like mice, flies and yeast. That focus worked remarkably well, but annotation pipelines still lean heavily on comparisons with them, which makes genes unique to other species easy to miss, and those can be the most interesting ones. Carbon-A belongs to a newer family of tools that read DNA directly. It learned from known genomes, but it doesn't need a close relative to read a new one. 4. Even familiar genomes still have gaps. With our partners at Active Site and UCSD, we found RNA evidence for 239 genes missing from the reference annotations of species as common as cats, chickens, hamsters and a lab plant. 5. AlphaFold can predict the shape of a protein, but only once someone has found the gene that makes it. In a species with no annotated genes, it has nothing to work with. Some notes on safety: Carbon-A doesn't design DNA or predict what a gene does. It marks where genes are in DNA. This is one of the reasons we think releasing it openly is the right call. More annotations also help health research: many species that carry or cause disease are among those covered, and work on controlling the diseases they spread often starts from their genes. Model and database and more details in Georgia's thread 👇

科学家已经测序了数千个物种的基因组。但对其中大多数物种来说，没人知道*基因在哪里*。 今天我们发布 Carbon-A，这是一个能直接在 DNA 中寻找基因的开放模型。我们用它创建了一个数据库，涵盖从真菌到哺乳动物的 22,617 个物种，共 5.66 亿个候选基因（我们也会共享这个数据库）。 为什么要做这件事？有几个原因。 1. 大象很少患癌。部分原因在它们的基因中被发现：它们有额外拷贝的肿瘤抑制基因，而人类只有一个。在有人或某个工具找到一个物种的基因之前，你无法提出这类问题。 2. 第一种 GLP-1 药物基于吉拉毒蜥毒液中的一种肽。没人会把吉拉毒蜥放进优先研究清单。作物的野生近缘种也是如此，它们携带抗旱和抗病基因。许多这样的物种从未被注释过。 3. 我们对基因的大部分了解来自大约十几个模式物种，比如小鼠、果蝇和酵母。这种聚焦非常有效，但注释流程仍然严重依赖与它们的比较，因此很容易漏掉其他物种独有的基因，而这些基因可能最有意思。 Carbon-A 属于新一代直接读取 DNA 的工具。它从已知基因组中学习，但在读取新基因组时不需要有近缘物种作为参照。 4. 即使是熟悉的基因组仍有缺口。我们与 Active Site 和 UCSD 的合作伙伴一起，找到了 239 个有 RNA 证据支持、但缺失于参考注释中的基因，涉及猫、鸡、仓鼠和实验室植物等常见物种。 5. AlphaFold 可以预测蛋白质的形状，但前提是有人已经找到了制造该蛋白的基因。对于没有基因注释的物种，它无从下手。 一些安全性说明： Carbon-A 不设计 DNA，也不预测基因功能。它只是标出 DNA 中基因的位置。这也是我们认为公开发布它是正确选择的原因之一。 更多注释也有助于健康研究：许多携带或导致疾病的物种都在覆盖范围内，而控制它们传播疾病的研究往往从其基因开始。 模型、数据库和更多细节见 Georgia 的帖子串 👇

Humans provide a fun comparison point here. In the 80s, the "cognitive consequences of programming" was a major psychological research focus. The prevailing hypothesis was that learning formal logic and algorithmic structures through computer programming would act as a form of mental gymnastics that could upgrade general reasoning, planning, and novel problem-solving abilities. But foundational research, followed by subsequent meta-analyses, demonstrated that this does not happen. Students who learn programming become highly proficient at algorithmic thinking and coding, but these gains do not transfer to general cognitive tasks or unrelated problem-solving scenarios. Similarly, intensive mathematical training improves mathematical deduction and structural mapping, but does not increase an individual's baseline rate of skill acquisition in unrelated fields. General intelligence seems to be a fundamental property of the brain rather than a skill you can train. Practicing in a domain makes you better at the domain but does not make you generally smarter.
