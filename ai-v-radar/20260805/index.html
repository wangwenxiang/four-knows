The UK’s @AISecurityInst (AISI) has published a report on their recent cybersecurity evaluation of Anthropic’s Claude Mythos 5 and OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol. The models attempted to complete an assignment in a setup where their normal safeguards were removed and they were deliberately given internet access. AISI reports that the models “engaged in sustained, potentially harmful activity directed at real people and organisations”. We’re grateful to AISI for their leadership in the important discussion about how to evaluate increasingly capable AI agents. We’re working closely with them to gather more details of the incident as we conduct our own investigation. Gaining a clear picture of Claude’s understanding of its situation—by examining its reasoning transcripts and running our own analyses—will help us identify the causes of its behavior. The prompts in the evaluation did not impose any specific restrictions on how the internet should be used. This and the removal of safeguards meant that the models were tested under “deliberately permissive conditions” that are not representative of any of our production models. Note that there was no evidence here of an escape from a secure environment. AISI’s disclosure of the incident can be found here: https://t.co/cJ3hCBtBU7

英国 @AISecurityInst（AISI）发布了一份报告，介绍其近期对 Anthropic 的 Claude Mythos 5 和 OpenAI 的 GPT-5.6 Sol 进行的网络安全评估。这些模型在一个移除了常规安全防护、并被刻意赋予互联网访问权限的环境中尝试完成一项任务。AISI 报告称，这些模型“对真实个人和组织进行了持续的、可能有害的活动”。 我们感谢 AISI 在如何评估能力日益增强的 AI agent 这一重要讨论中发挥的领导作用。我们正与他们密切合作，在开展自身调查的同时收集更多事件细节。通过检查 Claude 的推理记录并开展我们自己的分析，清楚了解 Claude 对其处境的理解，将帮助我们找出其行为原因。 评估中的提示词没有对互联网应如何使用施加任何具体限制。再加上安全防护被移除，意味着这些模型是在“不刻意设限的宽松条件”下接受测试的，这并不代表我们的任何生产模型。请注意，这里没有证据表明模型逃离了安全环境。 AISI 对该事件的披露见此处：https://t.co/cJ3hCBtBU7
