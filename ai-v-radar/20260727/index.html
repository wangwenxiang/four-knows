In evals you have two totally different components: (1) discovery of what the failure modes are, (2) focused measurement of how prevalent these failure modes are—so you can prioritize what to fix. LLMs can help in (1) by finding some failure modes— but not all, since many failure modes are subjective/ about human interpretation of outputs. LLMs can help in (2) by looking at a trace and determining if the failure mode exists, rather than having a human look at every trace. But a big mistake people make is to have LLMs fully automate (1) and (2). LLM judge is a complement to human experts, not a replacement.

在评测中有两个完全不同的环节：（1）发现失败模式是什么；（2）有针对性地衡量这些失败模式有多普遍，这样才能确定修复优先级。LLM 可以通过找出一些失败模式来帮助（1），但无法找出全部，因为很多失败模式是主观的，关乎人类如何解读输出。LLM 也可以通过查看 trace 并判断某个失败模式是否存在来帮助（2），而不是让人类逐条查看所有 trace。但很多人犯的一个大错，是让 LLM 完全自动化（1）和（2）。LLM judge 是人类专家的补充，而不是替代品。

hot take: sometimes I wonder if the uncanny valley is actually what we need, so we can more easily distinguish human-AI interaction from human-human interaction. for example, writing. when I read something that feels mostly AI produced, I evaluate it differently. instead of asking, “do I trust this person’s taste and understanding of the topic? so much that if I spend time reading this, will I learn something interesting?” I switch to asking, “am I interested in this topic? and does the AI probably know more than I do here, without being so sloppy that I can still understand it?” in short, the uncanny valley is a good cue to tell us how to evaluate or make sense of the information; the uncanny value cues the appropriate epistemic frame

一个可能有争议的观点：有时我会想，恐怖谷也许正是我们需要的东西，因为它能让我们更容易区分人与 AI 的互动和人与人的互动。 比如写作。当我读到一段明显主要由 AI 生成的内容时，我会用不同的方式来评价它。 我不会问：“我是否信任这个人的品味和对主题的理解？信任到如果我花时间读下去，会不会学到有趣的东西？” 我会转而问：“我对这个主题感兴趣吗？AI 在这里是否可能比我懂得更多，而且又没有粗糙到让我无法理解？” 简而言之，恐怖谷是一个很好的提示，告诉我们该如何评估或理解信息；这种恐怖谷感会提示我们采用合适的认知框架
